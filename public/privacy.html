Privacy policy

Last updated 21 November 2026

The short version: Unbork has no accounts, sets no cookies, runs no analytics and does not keep the addresses you check or the reports it produces. The one thing we keep for a free check is a count of how many checks were run each day, with nothing attached to it. If you pay for a full report we keep one more record, for seven days, and it holds a one-way hash of the address rather than the address itself. Both are described below.

Who runs Unbork

Unbork is run by [operator’s legal name and postal address, to be added]. For anything about your data, write to [contact email, to be added].

What happens when you run a check

When you press Check my site, the address you typed is sent to our checking function. The function opens the public pages and files at that address, the same way a browser would, builds the report and sends it back to your browser. The address and the report are held in memory only while the check runs. We do not write them to a database, a log or anywhere else.

The check reads only what the site you entered already shows to the public. If that public content happens to contain personal data, it is processed for the few seconds the check takes and is not kept.

The one number we keep

When a check finishes we add one to a count for that day. The stored value is a list of dates and two numbers against each: checks that produced a report, and pastes we could not check at all. That is the whole of it. No address, no IP, no browser, no country, no clock time, no identifier, nothing that could be traced back to you or to the site you checked, by us or by anyone who got hold of the file.

We keep it for one reason: without it we cannot tell whether anybody is using Unbork. You can read the same numbers we see at /api/stats. The counts are held by Netlify in the United States and the last 90 days are kept.

What our host processes

Unbork is hosted by Netlify, Inc. in the United States. To deliver the site, protect it from abuse and limit how many checks one visitor can run per minute, Netlify processes standard request data, including your IP address, browser type and the time of the request. Netlify does this as our service provider under its own privacy policy. This means that data is handled in the United States.

Our legal basis

Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, we rely on legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)): running the check you asked for, and keeping the service secure. We do not use your data for marketing, profiling or advertising, and we do not sell or share it.

Cookies and tracking

None. Unbork sets no cookies, loads no analytics or advertising tools and loads no fonts or scripts from other companies. That is why there is no cookie banner.

One small exception, which is not tracking and not a cookie: if you start a payment, your browser remembers the address you were checking for the rest of that tab, so that coming back from the payment page can show you the right report. It is held in your own browser, it is never sent to us except as part of the check you ask for, and closing the tab removes it.

How long we keep data

Unbork keeps nothing about you or your site after a free check finishes. The only things kept are the daily count described above, for 90 days, and, if you pay for a full report, the three-part record described under paid reports, for seven days. Request data held by Netlify is kept according to Netlify’s own policy.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask what data we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, and to object to how it is used. Because the only thing Unbork stores is a count that is not about anyone, in most cases there will be nothing to give you, but write to [contact email, to be added] and we will answer. If you are in the EU or UK you can also complain to your data protection authority.

Children

Unbork is meant for people who build and run websites, and is not aimed at children.

If you pay for a full report

Paid reports are not on sale yet. What follows is how the payment part is already built, so that it is published before anybody can be charged rather than after.

Who takes the payment. Razorpay. When you choose to unlock a report we ask Razorpay to create a payment link and send you to Razorpay’s own page to pay. Your card or bank details go to Razorpay and never reach us: we never see them and we could not store them if we wanted to. Razorpay handles them under its own privacy policy. We do not ask Razorpay to email or text you anything.

What Razorpay is told. An amount, a currency, and one reference. The reference is the one-way hash described below, so Razorpay is not told which site you checked. We do not send it your name, email address or phone number, because we do not have them.

What we store, and for how long. One record per paid report, for seven days, holding exactly three things: a one-way hash of the address you checked, the payment reference Razorpay gives us, and the date the unlock runs out. There is no account, no email address, no name, no copy of your report and no record of what the report said. The hash is made with a secret that is held only in our server’s settings and is not stored alongside it, so the file on its own cannot be turned back into your address. We keep the record so that your report stays unlocked, and so that you can re-run the same check free for seven days to confirm each fix worked. Once it runs out it unlocks nothing, and it is deleted the next time anything asks about that address.

Refunds. If you ask for a refund we process it through Razorpay, which needs the payment reference above. The refund policy is at /refunds.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top will change with it.
